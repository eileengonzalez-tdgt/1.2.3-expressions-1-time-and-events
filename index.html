01 · Encounter: Let's Talk About You

Warm up with activities you already know. There are no wrong preferences!

Step 1: Choose how you feel about at least four activities.

Step 2 · Talk to a partner.

Which activity would you choose for a free Saturday? Why? Ask your partner: “What about you?”

Stretch: Tell your partner one activity you would never choose and explain why.

02 · Clarify: Express Yourself

Notice how people share preferences and keep a conversation moving.

Read: A conversation between coworkers

Read the conversation with a partner. Take turns playing Alex and Sam. Notice how they explain preferences, react, and ask follow-up questions.

Alex: Hey, Sam! How was your weekend? Did you do anything interesting?
Sam: It was great, actually! I went downtown to take some photos. I’m really into photography, especially street photography.
Alex: That sounds fun! What do you enjoy most about taking pictures?
Sam: I love capturing little moments that people usually miss. It also helps me relax after a busy week at work. What about you? What do you like doing in your free time?
Alex: I’m more into sports. I usually play basketball with my friends on Saturdays. I enjoy being active, but I’m not a big fan of running alone.
Sam: Really? I don’t mind running, but I prefer walking around the city. I like exploring new places because I always find something interesting to photograph.
Alex: I get that! I’m interested in exploring the city too, although I don’t know much about photography. Do you ever take pictures of people playing sports?
Sam: Sometimes! Actually, I’d love to try photographing a basketball game. Is it difficult to get good action shots?
Alex: Probably, because everyone moves so quickly! Why don’t you come to our game next Saturday? You could take some photos, and I could introduce you to my friends.
Sam: That sounds like a great idea! I’d rather take photos than play basketball, but I’d definitely enjoy watching the game.
Alex: Perfect! I’ll send you the details. By the way, do you prefer morning or afternoon activities?
Sam: Afternoons, definitely. I hate waking up too early on weekends!
Alex: Same here! Looks like we have more in common than we thought.

Talk about the dialogue

  1. What does Sam enjoy doing?
  2. Why does Sam enjoy it?
  3. What activity does Alex prefer?
  4. What do Alex and Sam have in common?
  5. Which expression shows Alex is neutral about walking?

Quick structure guide

ExpressionPatternExample
Like / loveto + base verb OR verb-ingI love to cook. / I love cooking.
Enjoy / don't mindverb-ingI enjoy cooking.
Interested in / intonoun OR verb-ingI'm interested in learning.
Prefernoun / verb-ing + to + noun / verb-ingI prefer reading to watching TV.
Conversation toolkit: React → Ask → Expand

React: “Really?” / “That sounds fun!” / “Me too!”
Ask: “What do you like about it?” / “How often do you do that?”
Expand: “I enjoy it because…” / “For example…” / “What about you?”

Say it aloud: “I really enjoy cooking because it helps me relax.” Practice stressing really enjoy and using a friendly rising tone in “What about you?”

03 · Remember: Build Better Responses

10 short tasks. Check your answers and try again if needed.

Speaking bridge: Choose two corrected sentences and say them aloud. Add a reason using because and ask your partner “What about you?”

04 · Internalize: Find Your Match

Partner interview: don't stop after one answer. Listen, react, and ask follow-up questions.

Round 1 · Interview (5 min)

  1. What do you enjoy doing after work or class?
  2. What hobby are you interested in trying?
  3. Is there an activity you don't really like? Why?
  4. Do you prefer staying in or going out on weekends?

Follow up: Why? How often? What got you interested? What about you?

Round 2 · Compare (5 min)

Switch roles. Find:

  • One interest you share
  • One preference that's different
  • One surprising detail

Report back (3 min): “We both enjoy… but my partner prefers… because…”

05 · Fluent Use: Real-Life Conversations

Practice both scenarios: 6 minutes each, then 3 minutes for feedback and reflection.

Success criteria: Use at least 3 different preference expressions, give 2 reasons, ask 2 follow-up questions, and respond to your partner naturally.
